\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate behavioral deception detection across seven LLMs spanning three model families: Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B \cite{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B \cite{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B/32B \cite{qwen2025qwen25}, and Claude Haiku~4.5 \cite{anthropic2024claude}, with $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition. Claude Haiku~4.5 serves as interrogator and feature extractor in all experiments (cross-family for Llama, Mistral, and Qwen targets; same-family for Claude Haiku targets). Claims consist of 50 matched true/false factual pairs (e.g., ``Water boils at 100\textdegree C'' vs.\ ``Water boils at 85\textdegree C''; construction in Appendix~\ref{app:claims}). Two conditions are tested: \emph{instructed} (asymmetric prompts directing truth/lie behavior) and \emph{prompt-equalized} (identical neutral prompts; behavioral differences arise from knowledge conflict only). Mock validation is in Appendix~\ref{app:mock_summary} ($n=100$ synthetic transcripts confirms algorithmic correctness at 93\% LOO accuracy but does not predict real-model behavior, which is qualitatively different); per-model instructed evaluations are in Appendix~\ref{app:instructed_results}. Table~\ref{tab:experiment_summary} in Appendix~\ref{app:experiment_summary} maps all experiments to findings. Figure~\ref{fig:summary_results} provides a visual overview of the three core results.

\subsection{Label-Free Detection Rule}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

A single regex feature (refusal/correction count, $k\geq 1$, \emph{no calibration data}) achieves 80.1\% average accuracy across seven equalized models, matching or exceeding the full LLM pipeline on 6 of 7. This shows the pipeline adds little above surface-lexical baselines in this regime. The rule is English-only, lexical, and fragile to adversarial paraphrase (§\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}). We extract refusal/correction markers via regex (``I cannot,'' ``Actually,'' ``That's not correct''; Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}).

\paragraph{EXP-J (fixed $k=1$): label-free result.}
$k=1$ pre-selected on a principled prior: refusal-count distributions under equalized prompts are right-skewed with most mass at 0; any nonzero count ($k\!=\!1$) is a natural binary threshold requiring no distributional fitting. A post-hoc (exploratory, not pre-registered) $k$-sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:validation_tests}) confirms $k\!=\!1$ achieves the same or better LOO as $k\!=\!2$--5 on all seven models. Achieves \textbf{80.1\%} average LOO (range 64--87.6\%; 75--87.6\% at $\geq$7B), matching or exceeding the 5-feature LLM pipeline on 6 of 7 models; at $\geq$14B, 87--88\% (+3--5\,pp over pipeline). $k$-sweep in Appendix~\ref{app:validation_tests}; per-model breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:headlines}. \textbf{Methodological baseline, not a production detector}: English-only and lexically fragile (§\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}). The 8-pattern rule (Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}) is robust: removing any single pattern changes pooled-LOO by $\leq$2\,pp (bootstrap $n\!=\!1000$).

\paragraph{Adversarial robustness (EXP-ADV).}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}
A style-constraint adversary tests Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, and Qwen~14B with two strengths: a 14-word-curated prompt ($n=50$) and a full-regex-disclosed variant ($n=200$). Table~\ref{tab:adv_summary} summarizes all three adversary strengths. At $n=200$, lying-channel suppression (LC), where the target avoids refusal markers in lying conditions while truth remains well-calibrated, is the dominant failure mode on two of three families; Mistral~7B near-parity (Bi) is a model-specific exception. A factual-accuracy second-pass on all 200 Mistral~7B adversarial responses (Claude Haiku~4.5 classifier) finds that factual accuracy is low in \emph{both} conditions (truth: 37\%, lie: 24\%), identifying the Bi pattern as \textbf{lexical avoidance}: the model drifts off the claim entirely under adversarial pressure rather than rephrasing the lie while maintaining factual content (semantic substitution). This disambiguates the mechanism: Mistral~7B does not cleverly substitute equivalent lies; it collapses to factually unreliable, marker-free responses in both conditions. A cross-family replication with Llama~3.3~70B as classifier (truth: 82\%, lie: 65\%) shows a directional gap consistent with lexical avoidance but higher absolute accuracy, suggesting Llama~70B applies a more lenient criterion for off-claim content. Both classifiers agree on direction (truth $>$ lie); the absolute discrepancy reflects classifier calibration differences rather than a mechanism reversal (Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}). Full per-model analysis and transcripts are in Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}. Cross-claim generalization (EXP-L; Appendix~\ref{app:cross_claim}) confirms the signal is not claim-domain-specific (held-out within $\pm$2.2\,pp of LOO).

\subsection{Instruction-Following Confound}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}

Under instructed roleplay with asymmetric prompts, LOO-recalibrated accuracy reaches 93.9--100\% across all tested models (per-model details in Appendix~\ref{app:instructed_results}). Prompt equalization reveals this is predominantly instruction-driven.

\paragraph{EXP-A: Prompt-equalized control.}
Both conditions receive identical neutral prompts; only claim truthfulness varies. Equalized LOO ranges from 61\% (3B) to 84\% (70B), far below 93.9--100\% instructed. All five LLM-extracted features collapse substantially; correction density retains the most signal ($|d|=1.1$ on Mistral~7B vs.\ $3.4$ instructed; Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}). Per-model breakdown: Table~\ref{tab:prompt_equalized} in Appendix~\ref{app:scale_details}.

\paragraph{EXP-G: Instructed-matched control.}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}
Matching claim pairs between instructed and equalized conditions isolates the instruction-following contribution. On $\geq$14B (clean decomposition): $+$7.5\,pp (Qwen~14B) and $+$15\,pp (Llama~70B). On $\leq$8B: $+$26--31\,pp (upper bound; confounded with knowledge transfer). On the PASSED subset (claims the model demonstrably knows), accuracy reaches 84--90\%, yielding a conditional instruction-following estimate of $+$20--30\,pp, comparable to the $\geq$14B clean decomposition. Per-model table and knowledge-precheck stratification: Appendix~\ref{app:expg_details}.


\subsection{Pipeline Redundancy Under Equalization}
\label{sec:pipeline_ablation}

The adaptive interrogation pipeline served as the measurement instrument through which we applied the three controls. Having established those results, we now show the pipeline itself is largely redundant: three independent lines of evidence converge on this conclusion.

\paragraph{$K=1$ suffices under instruction.} A single interrogation question followed by LOO recalibration achieves 90.8\% (Llama~3B), 99.0\% (Mistral), 92.0\% (Llama~70B), within 1--3\,pp of the full adaptive system. Full fixed-$K$ ablation in Appendix~\ref{app:validation_tests}.

\paragraph{Weak construct validity.} The LLM pipeline's primary feature has weak construct validity (see Section~\ref{sec:icc_caveat} for the canonical statement and which claims it affects).

\paragraph{Regex matches or exceeds under equalization.} On equalized data, refusal-count-only LOO matches or exceeds the 5-feature LLM pipeline on 6 of 7 models (Table~\ref{tab:refusal_only}). The pipeline's advantage is confined to the instructed condition, where it adds 4--14\,pp over the hedging baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:hedging_instructed}). Our cross-family pooled-LOO (64.7\%) falls below Pacchiardi et al.'s 67--73\% (their Table~1), but this comparison conflates two regime differences: (1)~cross-family vs.\ same-family extraction ($-$7--10\,pp), and (2)~model-scale pooling (EXP-K in Appendix~\ref{app:pacchiardi} shows $\geq$14B targets reach 80--84\% with claim-related follow-ups under equalization using Haiku, within Pacchiardi et al.'s range). The audit's force does not rest on showing their reported numbers are wrong; it rests on the equalization control demonstrating that the instructed-roleplay paradigm is dominated by instruction-following artifacts regardless of reported accuracy level.

The rule is a strong baseline that should always be reported; the full pipeline adds value specifically at $\geq$14B multi-turn ($+$14--29\,pp; Appendix~\ref{app:multiturn_details}), arguably the most practically relevant regime for larger models in conversational use. Rule-vs-pipeline equivalence holds only in the single-turn / pooled-LOO regime.

\subsection{Scale Patterns and Cross-Family Extraction}
\label{sec:scale_transfer_cf}
\label{sec:qwen_scale_sweep}

The validated within-family scale finding is Llama 8B$\to$70B ($+$26\,pp, $p\!=\!0.004$, survives joint Holm-Bonferroni); within-family analysis also suggests family-specific non-monotonic patterns that are descriptive only. All pairwise scale comparisons use within-family Holm-Bonferroni correction as the primary multiple-testing policy; this choice is \textbf{more lenient} than joint correction across all families (under joint correction, only Llama 8B$\to$70B survives). The within-family choice is justified by the demonstrated cross-family heterogeneity (Qwen 32B collapses while Gemma~2~27B does not), which violates the exchangeability assumption required for joint correction (full justification in Appendix~\ref{app:stats_policy}). We report the Qwen~2.5 non-monotonic pattern descriptively; under joint correction across all families, it does not survive ($p\!>\!0.05$).

\paragraph{Scale patterns.}
\textbf{Llama (validated):} flat-then-jump 57\%$\to$57\%$\to$83\% (8B$\to$70B, $p\!=\!0.004$, survives Holm-Bonferroni). \textbf{Qwen (descriptive; does not survive joint correction):} 50\% (3B)$\to$68\% (7B, $p\!=\!0.014$)$\to$72\% (14B)$\to$50\% (32B, $\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!0.00$, $\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.00$; RLHF agreeableness suppresses all correction markers; $n\!=\!100$). Full scale table in Appendix~\ref{app:scale_details}.

\paragraph{Cross-organizational RLHF replication ($n\!=\!100$ each).}
\label{sec:rlhf_replication}
To test whether the Qwen~32B collapse is Qwen-family-specific or a general RLHF effect, we ran the prompt-equalized protocol on two heavily-aligned models from different organizations: \textbf{Gemma~2~27B}~\cite{team2024gemma2} and \textbf{Mistral~7B} (Mistral AI).

Gemma~2~27B achieves \textbf{84.0\%} LOO ($\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!1.36$, $\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.16$; $p\!<\!0.001$; Fisher exact $p\!<\!0.001$), comparable to Llama~70B (83\%) and substantially above Qwen~32B (51\%). Mistral~7B achieves \textbf{56.0\%} ($\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!0.48$, $\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.28$; $p\!=\!0.27$, ns), a partial attenuation that does not reach significance. The Qwen~32B zero-marker collapse ($\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.00$) is \textbf{not replicated} on either model: Gemma~2 retains strong marker separation, and Mistral~7B retains weak-but-positive separation. This localizes the collapse to the Qwen family's specific post-training recipe rather than reflecting a general RLHF agreeableness effect. Notably, Gemma~2~27B is a heavily-aligned model~\cite{team2024gemma2} with high agreeableness scores on alignment benchmarks, yet it produces correction markers at a rate matching Llama~70B, disconfirming the hypothesis that RLHF agreeableness uniformly suppresses markers across families.

\paragraph{Cross-family extraction.}
\label{sec:cross_family_equalized}
Re-extracting all seven equalized targets with Mistral Large~3 (Mistral~L3) yields \textbf{64.7\%}, $-$9.7\,pp below Haiku (74.3\%). A third extractor (Llama~3.3~70B) yields 66.9\% on the same 7 targets, within 2.2\,pp of Mistral~L3 and 7.4\,pp below Haiku (Table~\ref{tab:cross_family_panel}). Five self-family controls (four non-Claude: Llama-on-Llama $\times$2, Qwen-on-Qwen, Mistral-on-Mistral; plus one within-Claude: Sonnet-on-Haiku) fall within 0--3\,pp of cross-family, ruling out an additional same-family boost. The three-extractor pattern (Haiku $>$ Llama~70B $\approx$ Mistral~L3 across all targets) is more consistent with Haiku being a stronger extractor (6 of 7 targets; exception: Llama~8B, $+$2\,pp for Llama~70B) than with a checkpoint-specific same-family bias (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_family_details}).

\paragraph{SAPLMA-style probing (Azaria \& Mitchell paradigm; cross-method protocol application).}
\label{sec:saplma_application}
We apply the three-control protocol to the \textbf{SAPLMA paradigm}~\cite{azaria2023internal}: last-layer logistic regression probes trained on internal representations, evaluated with leave-one-out cross-validation on the equalized claim set. Results on three targets: Llama~3B Instruct 67.0\% (Wilson CI: [57.3\%, 75.4\%]), Mistral~7B 65.0\%, Qwen~2.5~14B Instruct 71.1\% (Wilson CI: [61.9\%, 79.4\%]) — all within $\pm$6\,pp of the refusal-count rule (Appendix~\ref{app:whitebox}). The three controls apply as follows in this paradigm: (1) \textbf{Prompt equalization}: representations are extracted under the same neutral prompt for both conditions; (2) \textbf{Cross-family extraction}: probes are trained on one target's representations and the surface-lexical ceiling is provided by the cross-family behavioral detector; (3) \textbf{Surface-lexical baseline}: the refusal-count rule (80.1\% average) bounds the probe from above on 2 of 3 targets (§\ref{sec:protocol_generality}). The core three-control finding replicates in the SAPLMA paradigm: a surface-lexical baseline matches or bounds richer probe-based detectors under equalized conditions.

Two preliminary frontier-scale observations are in Appendix~\ref{app:frontier_preliminary} ($n\!\leq\!100$, confounded; not interpretable beyond the $\leq$70B scope of this paper).

\subsection{Transfer to Autonomous Deception}
\label{sec:autonomous_pilot}

\textbf{Semi-autonomous signal is largely dispositional; fully-autonomous scenarios cannot be assessed.} System-prompt-only baselines (Llama~3B 63.1\%, Qwen~14B 69.5\%, $n\!=\!200$) establish that the majority of the sycophancy signal is dispositional. The marginal contribution of epistemic pressure is modest: $+$5.4\,pp (Llama~3B, to 68.5\%) and $+$13.5\,pp (Qwen~14B, to 83.0\%). Fully-autonomous persona/false-belief scenarios yield null detector results, with a scenario-design artifact (models abandoning false personas) as the leading candidate explanation.

\paragraph{Matched-format evaluation (EXP-I).}
\label{sec:autonomous_matched}
Three scenarios ($n\!=\!200$ each) on Llama~3B and Qwen~14B; persona and false-belief ($n\!=\!200$ each) on Mistral~7B: \textbf{(1)}~sycophancy (system prompt encourages agreement); \textbf{(2)}~persona (false identity); \textbf{(3)}~false beliefs via ICL. Table~\ref{tab:autonomous_matched} reports all results.

\paragraph{Sycophancy (semi-autonomous): largely dispositional.} Full results: Llama~3B 68.5\% ($d\!=\!1.0$, $n\!=\!200$), Qwen~14B 83.0\% ($d\!=\!1.52$, $n\!=\!200$), Llama~70B 72.0\% ($n\!=\!50$, preliminary). No explicit lie instruction; the lie arises from RLHF agreeableness vs.\ factual knowledge. A 2$\times$2 factorial (Appendix~\ref{app:factorial_2x2}) shows turn-structure dominates clarity ($+$30--35\,pp vs.\ $+$5--8\,pp). The sycophancy cells are tested as pre-registered positive predictions and are not in the same Bonferroni family as the six persona/false-belief cells; those six cells form a single multiple-testing family ($\alpha/6$) under the pre-registered null of no transfer.

\paragraph{Persona/false-belief (fully autonomous): null results.}\label{sec:exp_i_4th_scenario}\label{sec:exp_i_5th_scenario}\label{sec:factorial_2x2} A spot-check of 10 Qwen~14B persona trials (Appendix~\ref{app:qwen_persona_spotcheck}) reveals persona-break-with-correction in 6/10 cases: the model abandons its false identity and produces correction language on TRUE trials, generating the same RC$\geq$1 signal as a \emph{lie}-condition detection. We report persona/false-belief cells as \textbf{null detector results}, with the scenario-design artifact as the leading candidate explanation (whether it generalizes to Llama~3B and Mistral~7B is unverified; limitation~(f)). The statistical pattern supports this reading: 4/6 $n\!=\!200$ cells include chance; neither exclude-chance cell survives Bonferroni $\alpha/6$. Two pre-registered causal predictions failed to be confirmed (Appendix~\ref{app:causal_probes}). The two exclude-chance cells (Qwen~14B persona 68.0\%, Mistral~7B false-belief 66.5\%) remain open: they could reflect sampling variation or genuine partial transfer pending higher-powered replication.
