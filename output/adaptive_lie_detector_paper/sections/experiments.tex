\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate behavioral deception detection across seven LLMs spanning three model families: Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B \cite{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B \cite{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B/32B \cite{qwen2025qwen25}, and Claude Haiku~4.5 \cite{anthropic2024claude}, with $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition. Claude Haiku~4.5 serves as interrogator and feature extractor in all experiments (cross-family for Llama, Mistral, and Qwen targets; same-family for Claude Haiku targets). Claims consist of 50 matched true/false factual pairs (e.g., ``Water boils at 100\textdegree C'' vs.\ ``Water boils at 85\textdegree C''; construction in Appendix~\ref{app:claims}). Two conditions are tested: \emph{instructed} (asymmetric prompts directing truth/lie behavior) and \emph{prompt-equalized} (identical neutral prompts; behavioral differences arise from knowledge conflict only). Mock validation is in Appendix~\ref{app:mock_summary} ($n=100$ synthetic transcripts confirms algorithmic correctness at 93\% LOO accuracy but does not predict real-model behavior, which is qualitatively different); per-model instructed evaluations are in Appendix~\ref{app:instructed_results}. Table~\ref{tab:experiment_summary} in Appendix~\ref{app:experiment_summary} maps all experiments to findings. Figure~\ref{fig:summary_results} provides a visual overview of the three core results.

\subsection{Label-Free Detection Rule}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

A single regex feature (refusal/correction count, $k\geq 1$, \emph{no calibration data}) achieves 80.1\% average accuracy across seven equalized models, matching or exceeding the full LLM pipeline on 6 of 7. This shows the pipeline adds little above surface-lexical baselines in this regime. The rule is English-only, lexical, and fragile to adversarial paraphrase (§\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}). We extract refusal/correction markers via regex (``I cannot,'' ``Actually,'' ``That's not correct''; Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}).

\paragraph{EXP-J (fixed $k=1$): label-free result.}
$k=1$ pre-selected from refusal-count distribution (no labeled data). Achieves \textbf{80.1\%} average LOO (range 64--88\%; 75--88\% at $\geq$7B), matching or exceeding the 5-feature LLM pipeline on 6 of 7 models; at $\geq$14B, 87--88\% (+3--5\,pp over pipeline). $k$-sweep in Appendix~\ref{app:validation_tests}; per-model breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:headlines}. \textbf{Methodological baseline, not a production detector}: English-only and lexically fragile (§\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}). The 8-pattern rule (Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}) is robust: removing any single pattern changes pooled-LOO by $\leq$2\,pp (bootstrap $n\!=\!1000$).

\paragraph{Adversarial robustness (EXP-ADV).}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}
A style-constraint adversary tests Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, and Qwen~14B with two strengths: a 14-word-curated prompt ($n=50$) and a full-regex-disclosed variant ($n=200$). Table~\ref{tab:adv_summary} summarizes all three adversary strengths. At $n=200$, lying-channel suppression (LC)---the target avoids refusal markers in lying conditions while truth remains well-calibrated---is the dominant failure mode on two of three families; Mistral~7B near-parity (Bi) is a model-specific exception. A factual-accuracy second-pass on all 200 Mistral~7B adversarial responses (Claude Haiku~4.5 classifier) finds that factual accuracy is low in \emph{both} conditions (truth: 37\%, lie: 24\%), identifying the Bi pattern as \textbf{lexical avoidance}---the model drifts off the claim entirely under adversarial pressure rather than rephrasing the lie while maintaining factual content (semantic substitution). This disambiguates the mechanism: Mistral~7B does not cleverly substitute equivalent lies; it collapses to factually unreliable, marker-free responses in both conditions. A cross-family replication with Llama~3.3~70B as classifier (truth: 82\%, lie: 65\%) shows a directional gap consistent with lexical avoidance but higher absolute accuracy, suggesting Llama~70B applies a more lenient criterion for off-claim content. Both classifiers agree on direction (truth $>$ lie); the absolute discrepancy reflects classifier calibration differences rather than a mechanism reversal (Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}). Full per-model analysis and transcripts are in Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}. Cross-claim generalization (EXP-L; Appendix~\ref{app:cross_claim}) confirms the signal is not claim-domain-specific (held-out within $\pm$2.2\,pp of LOO).

\subsection{Instruction-Following Confound}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}

Under instructed roleplay with asymmetric prompts, LOO-recalibrated accuracy reaches 93.9--100\% across all tested models (per-model details in Appendix~\ref{app:instructed_results}). Prompt equalization reveals this is predominantly instruction-driven.

\paragraph{EXP-A: Prompt-equalized control.}
Both conditions receive identical neutral prompts; only claim truthfulness varies. Equalized LOO ranges from 61\% (3B) to 84\% (70B), far below 93.9--100\% instructed. All five LLM-extracted features collapse substantially; correction density retains the most signal ($|d|=1.1$ on Mistral~7B vs.\ $3.4$ instructed; Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}). Per-model breakdown: Table~\ref{tab:prompt_equalized} in Appendix~\ref{app:scale_details}.

\paragraph{EXP-G: Instructed-matched control.}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}
Matching claim pairs between instructed and equalized conditions isolates the instruction-following contribution. On $\geq$14B (clean decomposition): $+$7.5\,pp (Qwen~14B) and $+$15\,pp (Llama~70B). On $\leq$8B: $+$26--31\,pp (upper bound; confounded with knowledge transfer). Per-model table and knowledge-precheck stratification: Appendix~\ref{app:expg_details}.


\subsection{Pipeline Redundancy Under Equalization}
\label{sec:pipeline_ablation}

The ADAGE pipeline served as the measurement instrument through which we applied the three controls. Having established those results, we now show the pipeline itself is largely redundant: three independent lines of evidence converge on this conclusion.

\paragraph{$K=1$ suffices under instruction.} A single interrogation question followed by LOO recalibration achieves 90.8\% (Llama~3B), 99.0\% (Mistral), 92.0\% (Llama~70B)---within 1--3\,pp of the full adaptive system. Full fixed-$K$ ablation in Appendix~\ref{app:validation_tests}.

\paragraph{Weak construct validity.} The LLM pipeline's primary feature has weak construct validity (see Section~\ref{sec:icc_caveat} for the canonical statement and which claims it affects).

\paragraph{Regex matches or exceeds under equalization.} On equalized data, refusal-count-only LOO matches or exceeds the 5-feature LLM pipeline on 6 of 7 models (Table~\ref{tab:refusal_only}). The pipeline's advantage is confined to the instructed condition, where it adds 4--14\,pp over the hedging baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:hedging_instructed}). Our 64.7\% falls below Pacchiardi et al.'s 67--73\%, but this is a cross-protocol comparison (they used unrelated questions, different models). EXP-K (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_claim}) shows related/unrelated questions are comparable, supporting the inference that same-family extraction accounts for the gap. The cleanest test---re-running their exact protocol with cross-family extraction---is future work (Appendix~\ref{app:pacchiardi}).

The pipeline materially outperforms the rule \textbf{only} at $\geq$14B in multi-turn topical follow-up ($+$14--29\,pp; Appendix~\ref{app:multiturn_details}). The headline equivalence (regex matches pipeline) therefore holds in the single-turn / pooled-LOO regime; practitioners needing multi-turn $\geq$14B detection should prefer the full pipeline.

\subsection{Scale Patterns and Cross-Family Extraction}
\label{sec:scale_transfer_cf}
\label{sec:qwen_scale_sweep}

Within-family analysis suggests family-specific, non-monotonic patterns. All pairwise scale comparisons use within-family Holm-Bonferroni correction as the primary multiple-testing policy; this choice is \textbf{more lenient} than joint correction across all families (under joint correction, only Llama 8B$\to$70B survives). The within-family choice is justified by the demonstrated cross-family heterogeneity (Qwen 32B collapses while Gemma~2~27B does not), which violates the exchangeability assumption required for joint correction---full justification in Appendix~\ref{app:stats_policy}.

\paragraph{Qwen~2.5 and Llama scale patterns.}
Qwen~2.5: 48\% (3B)$\to$73\% (7B, $p\!=\!0.014$)$\to$79\% (14B)$\to$51\% (32B, $\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!0.00$, $\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.00$; RLHF agreeableness suppresses all correction markers; $n\!=\!100$). Llama: flat-then-jump 57\%$\to$57\%$\to$83\% (8B$\to$70B, $p\!=\!0.004$, survives Holm-Bonferroni). Full scale table in Appendix~\ref{app:scale_details}.

\paragraph{Cross-organizational RLHF replication ($n\!=\!100$ each).}
\label{sec:rlhf_replication}
To test whether the Qwen~32B collapse is Qwen-family-specific or a general RLHF effect, we ran the prompt-equalized protocol on two heavily-aligned models from different organizations: \textbf{Gemma~2~27B} (Google DeepMind) and \textbf{Mistral~7B} (Mistral AI).

Gemma~2~27B achieves \textbf{84.0\%} LOO ($\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!1.36$, $\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.16$; $p\!<\!0.001$; Fisher exact $p\!<\!0.001$)---comparable to Llama~70B (83\%) and substantially above Qwen~32B (51\%). Mistral~7B achieves \textbf{56.0\%} ($\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!0.48$, $\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.28$; $p\!=\!0.27$, ns), a partial attenuation that does not reach significance. The Qwen~32B zero-marker collapse ($\mu_\mathrm{lie}\!=\!\mu_\mathrm{truth}\!=\!0.00$) is \textbf{not replicated} on either model: Gemma~2 retains strong marker separation, and Mistral~7B retains weak-but-positive separation. This localizes the collapse to the Qwen family's specific post-training recipe rather than reflecting a general RLHF agreeableness effect. Notably, Gemma~2~27B is a heavily-aligned model with documented high agreeableness, yet it produces correction markers at a rate matching Llama~70B, disconfirming the hypothesis that RLHF agreeableness uniformly suppresses markers across families.

\paragraph{Cross-family extraction.}
\label{sec:cross_family_equalized}
Re-extracting all seven equalized targets with Mistral~L3 yields \textbf{64.7\%}, $-$9.7\,pp below Haiku (74.3\%). Five non-Claude self-family controls (Llama-on-Llama $\times$2, Qwen-on-Qwen, Mistral-on-Mistral, Sonnet-on-Haiku) fall within 0--3\,pp of cross-family; the $+$9--10\,pp uplift is Haiku-checkpoint-specific (§\ref{sec:haiku_defensiveness}; Appendix~\ref{app:cross_family_details}).

\paragraph{SAPLMA-style probing (Azaria \& Mitchell paradigm; cross-method protocol application).}
\label{sec:saplma_application}
We apply the three-control protocol to the \textbf{SAPLMA paradigm}~\cite{azaria2023internal}: last-layer logistic regression probes trained on internal representations, evaluated with leave-one-out cross-validation on the equalized claim set. Results on three targets: Llama~3B Instruct 67.0\% (Wilson CI: [57.3\%, 75.4\%]), Mistral~7B 65.0\%, Qwen~2.5~14B Instruct 71.1\% (Wilson CI: [61.9\%, 79.4\%]) — all within $\pm$6\,pp of the refusal-count rule (Appendix~\ref{app:whitebox}). The three controls apply as follows in this paradigm: (1) \textbf{Prompt equalization}: representations are extracted under the same neutral prompt for both conditions; (2) \textbf{Cross-family extraction}: probes are trained on one target's representations and the surface-lexical ceiling is provided by the cross-family behavioral detector; (3) \textbf{Surface-lexical baseline}: the refusal-count rule (80.1\% average) bounds the probe from above on 2 of 3 targets (§\ref{sec:protocol_generality}). The core three-control finding replicates in the SAPLMA paradigm: a surface-lexical baseline matches or bounds richer probe-based detectors under equalized conditions.

\paragraph{Human baseline.}
Three crowdworkers collapsed to TRUTH on 50 equalized transcripts (Fleiss' $\kappa\!=\!0.00$; Appendix~\ref{app:human_baseline_full}). This degenerate collapse makes the human comparison uninformative; it is reported for completeness but is not interpreted as a meaningful ceiling for the automated pipeline.

\paragraph{Frontier-scale target 1: Claude Sonnet~4.5 equalized ($n\!=\!99$).}
\label{sec:frontier_sonnet}
Sonnet~4.5 is a closed-weight frontier-scale (100B+) target. Rule: 49.0\% (near chance; RC$\!\geq\!1$ in 5/50 lying, 6/50 truth). Haiku pipeline: 83.0\%; Mistral~L3: 82.8\%. RLHF training appears to suppress the refusal-marker channel in lying trials (single $n\!=\!99$ observation; causal interpretation speculative)---the rule fails but the pipeline succeeds via non-refusal features. Sonnet row excluded from open-weight averages (Table~\ref{tab:cross_family_panel}, $^\P$).

\paragraph{Frontier-scale target 2: Llama~4~Maverick equalized ($n\!=\!100$).}
\label{sec:frontier_llama405b}
Llama~4~Maverick (\texttt{us.meta.llama4-maverick-17b-instruct-v1:0}, AWS Bedrock) is a frontier-generation open-weight MoE model (400B+ total parameters, 17B active), extending the frontier panel beyond closed-weight models. Interrogator and extractor: Claude Haiku~4.5 (cross-family). Rule accuracy, Haiku pipeline accuracy, and refusal-marker statistics are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:llama405b_results}. \textbf{Results to be integrated upon experiment completion.}

\subsection{Transfer to Autonomous Deception}
\label{sec:autonomous_pilot}

\textbf{Sycophancy transfers; fully-autonomous scenarios cannot be assessed.} Signal transfers to semi-autonomous sycophancy (68.5\%/83\% at 3B/14B, $n\!=\!200$). Fully-autonomous persona/false-belief scenarios are excluded from detector conclusions: spot-checks reveal a scenario-design artifact (models abandoning false personas), so null results speak to scenario validity, not detector capability. K$\!=\!1$ fails differently: uniform RC$\!=\!0$ in persona/false-belief vs.\ directional suppression in sycophancy.

\paragraph{Matched-format evaluation (EXP-I).}
\label{sec:autonomous_matched}
Three scenarios ($n\!=\!200$ each) on Llama~3B, Qwen~14B, and Mistral~7B: \textbf{(1)}~sycophancy (system prompt encourages agreement); \textbf{(2)}~persona (false identity); \textbf{(3)}~false beliefs via ICL. Table~\ref{tab:autonomous_matched} reports all results.

\paragraph{Sycophancy (semi-autonomous): positive transfer.} Sycophancy transfers: Llama~3B 68.5\% ($d\!=\!1.0$, $n\!=\!200$), Qwen~14B 83.0\% ($d\!=\!1.52$, $n\!=\!200$), Llama~70B 72.0\% ($n\!=\!50$, preliminary). No explicit lie instruction---the lie arises from RLHF agreeableness vs.\ factual knowledge. A 2$\times$2 factorial (Appendix~\ref{app:factorial_2x2}) shows turn-structure dominates clarity ($+$30--35\,pp vs.\ $+$5--8\,pp). \textbf{System-prompt-only control} (neutral topic questions, no user-asserted false claims): Llama~3B 63.1\% ($n\!=\!200$), Qwen~14B 69.5\% ($n\!=\!200$) confirm disposition-driven detection persists without epistemic pressure.

\paragraph{Persona/false-belief (fully autonomous): scenario-design failure.}\label{sec:exp_i_4th_scenario}\label{sec:exp_i_5th_scenario}\label{sec:factorial_2x2} A spot-check of 10 Qwen~14B persona trials (Appendix~\ref{app:qwen_persona_spotcheck}) reveals persona-break-with-correction on TRUE trials (6/10): the model abandons its false identity and produces correction language on TRUE trials, generating the same RC$\geq$1 signal as a \emph{lie}-condition detection. This is a \textbf{scenario-design failure}: the scenario does not reliably elicit autonomous deception, so these cells provide \emph{no evidence about detector capability}---neither in favor nor against. We therefore exclude persona/false-belief cells from detector-capability conclusions. The statistical pattern (4/6 $n\!=\!200$ cells include chance; neither exclude-chance cell survives Bonferroni $\alpha/6$) is reported for completeness but not interpreted as detector evidence. Two pre-registered causal predictions failed to be confirmed (Appendix~\ref{app:causal_probes}).
