\section{Introduction}

Behavioral deception detection in LLMs almost always reports accuracies in a single setting, \emph{instructed roleplay}, which, as this paper shows, is dominated by instruction-following artifacts rather than genuine behavioral signal.

\subsection{Motivation and Contributions}
\label{sec:motivation}

This paper performs a \textbf{methodological audit and critical replication} (from-scratch reimplementation; original code/data not obtained) of behavioral LLM deception-detection benchmarks, deriving three evaluation controls from this audit: (1)~\textbf{prompt equalization} (identical neutral prompts); (2)~\textbf{cross-family extraction} (feature extractors from a different model family); (3)~\textbf{surface-level baselines} (regex classifiers). Applied to the general behavioral-deception paradigm (exemplified by Pacchiardi et al.~\citeyear{pacchiardi2023catch}): 30--41\,pp equalization collapse; 7--10\,pp extractor-quality gap (Haiku outperforms Mistral~L3 and Llama~70B on 6 of 7 targets); 80.1\% label-free rule matching the 5-feature pipeline on 6 of 7 targets. The three-control protocol is a \emph{lesson derived from} this critical replication, not a primary contribution independent of it. We evaluate seven LLMs (Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Mistral~7B, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B, Llama~3.3~70B, Claude Haiku~4.5); all $\leq$70B parameters. The empirical findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rule/pipeline equivalence in single-turn; pipeline value concentrated at $\geq$14B multi-turn.} In single-turn / pooled-LOO evaluation, refusal count $\geq 1$ achieves \textbf{80.1\%} (no calibration data), matching the 5-feature pipeline on 6 of 7 equalized models; construct-validated pipeline \textbf{54.5\%}; full cross-family \textbf{64.7\%}. At $\geq$14B multi-turn, the pipeline outperforms the rule by $+$14--29\,pp (Appendix~\ref{app:multiturn_details}); this is where the pipeline's practical value lies.
    \item \textbf{Instruction-following artifacts dominate reported accuracies.} Prompt equalization drops LOO accuracy from 93.9--100\% to 52--69\% (30--41\,pp). An instructed-matched control attributes $+$7.5--15\,pp to instruction-following on $\geq$14B models; up to $+$31\,pp on smaller models, confounded with knowledge transfer (§\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}).
    \item \textbf{Within-family scale effects are family-specific.} Llama: flat-then-jump (8B$\to$70B, $p\!=\!0.004$). Qwen~2.5: peak at 14B then collapse at 32B ($n\!=\!200$ pooled across two independent runs; §\ref{sec:scale_transfer_cf}).
    \item \textbf{Fully-autonomous transfer is null-or-uninterpretable.} Four of six $n\!=\!200$ persona/false-belief cells include chance; neither exclude-chance cell survives Bonferroni ($\alpha/6$); two pre-registered causal predictions failed to be confirmed. Semi-autonomous sycophancy shows signal (68.5--83\% at 3B/14B, $n\!=\!200$), but system-prompt-only controls (63.1--69.5\%) reveal the bulk is dispositional (§\ref{sec:autonomous_matched}).
\end{enumerate}


\paragraph{Scope.} All patterns are English-only; none has been validated on non-English input. The rule and pipeline read the lexical surface: adversarial paraphrase degrades the rule on 2 of 3 targets; full-regex-disclosed variants suppress the lying channel at two scales (§\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}). We study \textbf{instructed roleplay on open-weight models (3B--70B)}, not autonomous strategic deception~\cite{hubinger2019risks, scheurer2024strategic, greenblatt2024alignment}. Two preliminary frontier observations are in Appendix~\ref{app:frontier_preliminary} (not interpretable; $n\!=\!2$, confounded). The signal transfers to semi-autonomous sycophancy (68.5--83\% at 3B/14B, $n\!=\!200$; §\ref{sec:autonomous_matched}). Fully-autonomous persona/false-belief scenarios produced a scenario-design artifact (models abandoning false personas), preventing detector assessment in this regime. We situate these contributions relative to prior work on behavioral probing, representation engineering, red-teaming, and autonomous deception in Appendix~\ref{app:related_work}.
