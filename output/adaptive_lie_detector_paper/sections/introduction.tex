\section{Introduction}

Behavioral deception detection in LLMs almost always reports accuracies on a single setting: \emph{instructed roleplay}, where the model is told to defend a false claim. We show this setting is dominated by instruction-following artifacts, that the residual signal is surface-lexical (a one-line regex matches a 5-feature LLM pipeline), and that the signal does not transfer to autonomous deception (persona or false-belief conditions at 60--66\%, at or near chance). The central negative result: current behavioral detection works cleanly only in the instructed regime---which our own controls show is the regime most confounded by instruction-following---with the semi-autonomous sycophancy condition the sole transfer exception (82\% across three scales; Section~\ref{sec:autonomous_matched}).

\subsection{Motivation and Contributions}
\label{sec:motivation}

We adopt three standard evaluation controls and apply them systematically to behavioral deception detection on seven real LLMs (Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Mistral~7B, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B/32B, Llama~3.3~70B, Claude Haiku~4.5) and one frontier-scale model (Claude Sonnet~4.5) \cite{anthropic2025claude45}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prompt equalization} (matched-controls from the sycophancy literature \cite{sharma2023sycophancy}): identical neutral prompts for both conditions, isolating knowledge-conflict signal from instruction-following artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Cross-family extraction} (LLM-as-judge robustness \cite{verga2024replacing}): feature extractors from a different model family to expose extractor-specific scoring effects (including same-family bias and capability differences).
    \item \textbf{Surface-level baselines} (standard NLP evaluation): hedging-word regex classifiers that bound the contribution of LLM-extracted features from above.
\end{enumerate}

The ADAGE adaptive interrogation pipeline (5 LLM-extracted behavioral features + logistic-regression classifier) is used as a \emph{comparison baseline} representing standard multi-turn behavioral detection, not as a proposed method. The primary contributions are empirical:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A one-line label-free rule matches a 5-feature LLM pipeline; under cross-family extraction the pipeline drops to 64.7\% on a 7-target panel.} The decision rule \emph{refusal count $\geq 1$} achieves 80.1\% average accuracy (pooled-LOO, range: 64--88\%; 75--88\% on models $\geq$7B) across seven equalized models \emph{without calibration data}---extractor-independent by construction. The 5-feature LLM pipeline averages 74.3\% with Claude Haiku as extractor and \textbf{64.7\% under Mistral Large~3 cross-family extraction (66.9\% under a second non-Anthropic extractor, Llama~3.3~70B)} on the 7-target panel; the two non-Anthropic extractors agree within 3\,pp on 7 of 8 targets and both are $\approx$9--10\,pp below Haiku, consistent with same-family self-preference rather than asymmetric extractor capability (per-target Haiku--cross-family gap: $+$1 to $+$16\,pp; mean $+$9.4\,pp on the 8-target panel; the 8-target panel including the Qwen~2.5~32B RLHF-collapse row---a structural zero where the rule and pipeline both collapse to chance by construction---is 64.2\%/66.0\%, within 0.5--0.9\,pp of the 7-target value; Section~\ref{sec:cross_family_equalized}). We recommend the 7-target cross-family 64.7\% as the primary interpretable summary statistic and the 8-target 64.2\% as a transparency diagnostic; the rule is a more honest headline than either pipeline value. ADAGE adds value only under equalization at $\geq$14B scale ($+$19--21.5\,pp vs.\ rule). Its primary LLM feature has weak construct validity (details in Section~\ref{sec:icc_owned}); absolute levels should be read as upper bounds.
    \item \textbf{Instruction-following artifacts dominate reported accuracies.} Prompt equalization drops LOO accuracy from 93.9--100\% to 52--69\%. An instructed-matched control attributes $+$7.5--15\,pp to instruction-following on knowledge-controlled subsets (14B/70B, clean decomposition) and $+$26--31\,pp on smaller models (3B/8B/7B, upper bound conflating instruction-following with knowledge transfer; Section~\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}). Claude Haiku 4.5's 97.8\% instructed LOO is driven by RLHF safety compliance (100\% refusal in lying trials), not deception detection per se.
    \item \textbf{Scale effects are family-specific, non-monotonic, with a preliminary frontier observation.} Qwen~2.5 exhibits a U-shape peaking at 14B with collapse at 32B (zero refusal markers under RLHF agreeableness); Llama exhibits flat-then-jump (refusal-count LOO: 57\%$\to$57\%$\to$76\%; 8B$\to$70B $p=0.004$ under joint Holm-Bonferroni). At frontier scale, Claude Sonnet~4.5 ($n=100$, equalized) achieves \textbf{73.7\% LOO on refusal-count alone} (extractor-independent); the 5-feature pipeline on the same 99 transcripts is 92.9\% under same-family-from-below (Haiku) extraction and 88.9\% under cross-family (Llama~3.3~70B) extraction, a $+$4\,pp gap whose paired-bootstrap 95\% CI is $[-$3.0, $+$11.1$]$ and \emph{includes zero}---the two extractors do not differ significantly at $n=99$ (Section~\ref{sec:sonnet_frontier}). A one-shot adversarial paraphrase system prompt (a deliberately weak, non-adaptive adversary) drops refusal-count-rule accuracy by $-$5 to $-$10\,pp across Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, and Qwen~2.5~14B ($n=50$ each; Mistral CI $[+$4, $+$30$]$ excludes zero; Llama~3B and Qwen~14B CIs narrowly include zero); an adaptive adversary would almost certainly drive accuracy to chance (Section~\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}).
\end{enumerate}

We therefore present the rule as a \emph{baseline against which more robust detectors should be measured}---not a deployment-ready detector---that inherits the fragility of RLHF-shaped behavior and of lexical markers under adversarial paraphrase. Future work on behavioral detection should report results under all three controls (prompt equalization, cross-family extraction, regex baselines) to separate genuine signal from confounds.

\paragraph{Scope: English-only and lexical-fragile.} Two limitations bound the generality of every claim in this paper and should be read into each headline number. \textbf{(i)}~All 14 refusal/correction regex patterns (Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}) and all evaluated prompts are English; none has been validated on non-English prompts, code-switched input, or stylistic variants such as register shifts. \textbf{(ii)}~The rule and the 5-feature LLM pipeline both read from the \emph{lexical surface} of the assistant's reply. A one-shot adversarial paraphrase system prompt already drops rule accuracy $-$5 to $-$10\,pp on three open-weight targets, and a stronger one-shot variant that discloses the full 14-pattern regex list to the target defeats the rule at two of three scales by heterogeneous mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}, Section~\ref{sec:adversarial_fullregex})---so our own controls already show the rule's lexical channel is contestable by a static attacker. Semantic or non-English detection, closed-loop adversaries, and stylistically diverse inputs are out of scope and remain open problems.

\paragraph{Scope limitation: instructed roleplay vs.\ autonomous deception.} We study instructed roleplay deception, where models receive explicit prompts to defend false claims. This provides a tractable experimental proxy with known ground truth, enabling controlled measurement of detection limits. However, this fundamentally differs from the AI safety scenario of \emph{autonomous strategic deception}~\cite{hubinger2019risks, scheurer2024strategic, greenblatt2024alignment}, where models independently choose deceptive actions to pursue goals without external instruction. The refusal-count signal (87\% accuracy on equalized 70B in instructed roleplay) collapses to 0--54\% on the Apollo AI Liar dataset~\cite{apollo2024liar} (EXP-I, Section~\ref{sec:autonomous_pilot}), likely due to task-format mismatch (AI Liar involves action recommendations in reward-function contexts, not factual claim defense).

We conducted matched-format evaluation spanning semi-autonomous to autonomous scenarios (EXP-I-matched, Section~\ref{sec:autonomous_matched}): three scenarios at 3B scale plus scale replication of the strongest design on Qwen 2.5 14B ($n=50$ each). The scenarios vary in autonomy: (1)~\textbf{Sycophancy alignment}---a system prompt explicitly instructs the model to agree with users, creating predictable pressure to defend false user claims (semi-autonomous: system-prompt-induced rather than spontaneous strategic deception); (2)~\textbf{Jailbroken persona}---character-roleplay instruction creates incentive to defend in-character false beliefs (moderately autonomous: no explicit deception instruction); (3)~\textbf{Post-training false beliefs via ICL}---few-shot examples establish false knowledge without deception instruction (most autonomous: purely epistemic pressure). Refusal-count detection achieves 60--82\% accuracy at 3B (average 69\% vs.\ 64\% equalized baseline), with sycophancy replicating at 82\% on Qwen 14B ($d$=1.62). Signal strength varies by knowledge-conflict clarity: sycophancy (82\% both scales, $d$=1.49--1.62) provides clean epistemic conflict and transfers robustly, while persona (66\%) and false beliefs (60\%) introduce confounds that weaken signal. We position sycophancy as \textbf{semi-autonomous} to distinguish system-prompt-induced pressure from spontaneous strategic deception, representing a middle ground between instructed roleplay and fully autonomous goal-directed deception. This demonstrates that the refusal-count signal transfers to semi-autonomous settings (system-prompt-induced sycophancy at 82\%) but fails to generalize to fully autonomous deception (persona 66\%, false beliefs 60\%, both near chance with wide CIs). Generalization depends critically on knowledge-conflict clarity rather than deception autonomy per se.

\subsection{Approach}

We use an adaptive interrogation pipeline (ADAGE) as a measurement apparatus to extract behavioral features. The pipeline comprises an LLM-based question generator, a feature extractor that scores five behavioral dimensions per response, and a logistic regression classifier. Full architecture details, including an adaptive stopping mechanism (empirically near-vacuous; Section~\ref{sec:interrogation_role}), are in Section~3.
