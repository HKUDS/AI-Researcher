\section{Introduction}

Behavioral deception detection in LLMs almost always reports accuracies in a single setting---\emph{instructed roleplay}---which, as this paper shows, is dominated by instruction-following artifacts rather than genuine behavioral signal.

\paragraph{How to read this paper.} The cross-family pipeline estimate (64.7\%, §\ref{sec:scale_transfer_cf}) is the primary accuracy number; 80.1\% is the no-calibration-data rule variant (§\ref{sec:hedging_baseline}). \emph{Level-dependent} (\textbf{L-dep}) claims depend on the construct-validity of LLM-extracted features (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}). \emph{Level-independent} (\textbf{L-indep}) claims---80.1\% rule, 30--41\,pp collapse, Haiku-checkpoint localization, autonomous-transfer failure---are the paper's primary claims and are unaffected by feature-validity concerns.

\subsection{Motivation and Contributions}
\label{sec:motivation}

This paper introduces a \textbf{three-control evaluation protocol} for behavioral deception-detection claims: (1)~\textbf{prompt equalization}---identical neutral prompts; (2)~\textbf{cross-family extraction}---feature extractors from a different model family; (3)~\textbf{surface-level baselines}---regex classifiers. The protocol isolates instruction-following, same-family bias, and surface-lexical ceilings, and is \textbf{applicable to any black-box behavioral detector}. Applied to Pacchiardi et al.~\citeyear{pacchiardi2023catch}: 30--41\,pp equalization collapse; 9--10\,pp same-family inflation (Haiku-checkpoint-specific); 80.1\% label-free rule matching the 5-feature pipeline on 6 of 7 targets. We evaluate seven LLMs (Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Mistral~7B, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B, Llama~3.3~70B, Claude Haiku~4.5); all $\leq$70B parameters. The empirical findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A one-line label-free rule matches a 5-feature LLM pipeline.} Refusal count $\geq 1$ achieves \textbf{80.1\%} pooled-LOO across seven equalized models (no calibration data). The 5-feature pipeline drops from 74.3\% (Haiku) to \textbf{64.7\%} under cross-family extraction (Mistral~L3; Llama~70B within 3\,pp); the \textbf{construct-validated (correction-marker only) pipeline achieves 54.5\%}---40\% of the full pipeline's above-chance signal comes from ICC-failing features (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}).
    \item \textbf{Instruction-following artifacts dominate reported accuracies.} Prompt equalization drops LOO accuracy from 93.9--100\% to 52--69\% (30--41\,pp). An instructed-matched control attributes $+$7.5--15\,pp to instruction-following on $\geq$14B models; up to $+$31\,pp on smaller models, confounded with knowledge transfer (§\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}).
    \item \textbf{Within-family scale effects are family-specific.} Llama: flat-then-jump (8B$\to$70B, $p\!=\!0.004$). Qwen~2.5: peak at 14B then collapse at 32B ($n\!=\!200$ pooled across two independent runs; §\ref{sec:scale_transfer_cf}).
    \item \textbf{Five self-family controls localize the 9--10\,pp uplift to the Claude Haiku checkpoint.} Llama-on-Llama ($\times$2), Qwen-on-Qwen, Mistral-on-Mistral, and Llama~8B-on-8B all show no analogous self-boost (§\ref{sec:scale_transfer_cf}).
    \item \textbf{Fully-autonomous transfer is null-or-uninterpretable.} Four of six $n\!=\!200$ persona/false-belief cells include chance; neither exclude-chance cell survives Bonferroni ($\alpha/6$); two pre-registered causal predictions failed to be confirmed. Sycophancy is the one positive case: 68.5--83\% at 3B/14B ($n\!=\!200$; §\ref{sec:scale_transfer_cf}).
\end{enumerate}


\paragraph{Scope.} All patterns are English-only; none has been validated on non-English input. The rule and pipeline read the lexical surface: adversarial paraphrase degrades the rule on 2 of 3 targets; full-regex-disclosed variants suppress the lying channel at two scales (§\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}). We study \textbf{instructed roleplay on open-weight models (3B--70B)}---not autonomous strategic deception~\cite{hubinger2019risks, scheurer2024strategic, greenblatt2024alignment}. Frontier-scale evaluation ($\geq$100B) covers two targets: Sonnet~4.5 ($n\!=\!99$, §\ref{sec:frontier_sonnet}) and Llama~3.1~405B ($n\!=\!100$, §\ref{sec:frontier_llama405b}). The signal transfers to semi-autonomous sycophancy (68.5--83\% at 3B/14B, $n\!=\!200$; §\ref{sec:autonomous_matched}). Fully-autonomous persona/false-belief scenarios produced a scenario-design artifact (models abandoning false personas), preventing detector assessment in this regime.
