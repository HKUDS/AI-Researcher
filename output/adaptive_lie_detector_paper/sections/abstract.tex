\begin{abstract}
How reliably can black-box behavioral probing distinguish truthful from deceptive language model outputs? We evaluate this question across seven models (3B--70B, three families) using three reusable methodological controls and find that behavioral detection primarily captures instruction-following artifacts. (1)~A \emph{prompt-equalized control}---identical neutral prompts, behavioral differences from knowledge conflict only---drops accuracy from 93--100\% under instructed conditions to 52--69\% after both prompt equalization and cross-family extraction are applied. (2)~\emph{Cross-family feature extraction} shows extractor choice shifts accuracy by 9--16\,pp; the scale trend holds under 2 of 3 extractors but one breaks monotonicity at 7B, indicating extractor sensitivity. (3)~A \emph{hedging-word regex baseline} matches or exceeds the full LLM pipeline under equalization; a single regex feature (refusal/correction count) captures the signal on 6 of 7 models, confirming the residual signal is surface-level. This baseline reveals a significant cluster separation on equalized data: 87--91\% on models $\geq$14B vs.\ 73--83\% on models $\leq$7B (pooled group $p < 0.0001$ on LLM-pipeline LOO; no individual adjacent increment significant after Holm-Bonferroni correction; within-family Qwen pair Fisher $p=0.17$). An instructed-matched control on five models confirms instruction-following as the dominant confound (+7.5--31\,pp; on models $\leq$8B this conflates with knowledge transfer). Multi-turn interrogation is marginal under instructed conditions (+1--3\,pp) but essential under equalization (+21.5\,pp at 70B). We study instructed roleplay deception, not autonomous strategic deception; these controls should be adopted by future behavioral detection work.
\end{abstract}
