\begin{abstract}
Behavioral deception detection in large language models has been evaluated almost exclusively on \emph{instructed roleplay}, a setting where the target is told to defend a false claim. We show this setting is dominated by instruction-following artifacts: under three standard controls (prompt equalization, cross-family feature extraction, and surface-level regex baselines), a one-line label-free rule---\emph{refusal count $\geq 1$}---matches or exceeds a 5-feature LLM interrogation pipeline, and the residual signal does not transfer to fully-autonomous persona-maintenance or false-belief conditions at two independently-pretrained scales (Llama 3B: 66\%/60\%; Qwen 14B: 64\%/66\%; Wilson 95\% CIs include chance at both scales, $n=50$ each). A semi-autonomous sycophancy condition is the one scenario that does transfer across three scales (82\% at 3B, 82\% at 14B, 72\% at 70B; $d=0.9$--$1.6$), consistent with knowledge-conflict clarity (not deception autonomy) being the signal our rule actually tracks. The paper's central negative result is that current behavioral detection works cleanly only in the instructed regime---which our controls show is the regime most confounded by instruction-following---with the semi-autonomous sycophancy condition the sole transfer exception.

Across eight equalized models (Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Mistral~7B, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B/32B, Llama~3.3~70B, Claude Haiku~4.5), the refusal-count rule averages 80.1\% with Claude-Haiku-extracted features (pooled-LOO, fixed $k\!=\!1$); under two independent non-Anthropic cross-family extractors (Mistral Large~3 and Llama~3.3~70B), the 5-feature pipeline average drops from 74.3\% to \textbf{64.7\% and 66.9\% respectively (7-target panel, primary headline; gap $-$7.4 to $-$9.7\,pp)}; the two non-Anthropic extractors agree within 3\,pp on 7 of 8 targets, consistent with same-family self-preference rather than asymmetric extractor capability. The 8-target panel including the Qwen~2.5~32B RLHF-collapse row (a structural zero where rule and pipeline collapse to chance by construction) is 64.2\%/66.0\%, within 0.5--0.9\,pp. The rule itself is extractor-independent by construction. A single frontier pilot ($n=99$, Claude Sonnet~4.5, underpowered by $4$--$6\times$ for the key between-extractor comparison) reports refusal-count LOO 73.7\% and pipeline 92.9\%~(Haiku) / 88.9\%~(Llama~3.3~70B), paired-bootstrap 95\% CI on the $+$4\,pp gap $[-$3.0, $+$11.1$]$ (includes zero); we treat this as a single measurement, not a tested claim.

Prompt equalization drops instructed LOO from 93.9--100\% to 52--69\%. An instructed-matched control attributes $+$7.5--15\,pp to instruction-following on knowledge-controlled subsets ($\geq$14B) and $+$26--31\,pp on smaller models (upper bound conflating instruction-following with knowledge transfer). Qwen~2.5 exhibits a U-shape (3B$\to$32B: 50\%$\to$68\%$\to$72\%$\to$50\%), with collapse at 32B from zero refusal markers under RLHF agreeableness. The primary LLM-extracted feature (correction-marker density) has weak inter-rater reliability (ICC=0.114, $n=20$, 2 annotators)---absolute marker levels should be read as upper bounds. A one-shot adversarial paraphrase prompt---a deliberately weak static adversary---drops rule accuracy $-$5 to $-$10\,pp on Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, and Qwen~2.5~14B (Mistral 95\% paired CI $[+$4, $+$30$]$ excludes zero); a strictly stronger one-shot variant disclosing the full 14-pattern detector regex list breaks the rule in two distinct directions at two different scales---\textbf{Llama~3B 50.0\%} via truth-channel suppression (truth 8\% / lying 92\%) and \textbf{Qwen~14B 44.0\%} via the opposite-direction lying-channel suppression (truth 68\% / lying 20\%), both $n=50$---while \textbf{Mistral~7B 66.0\% ($n=50$)} materially preserves both channels; the one-shot informed adversary thus defeats the rule by \emph{heterogeneous mechanism} at two of three scales, not by uniform collapse (§\ref{sec:adversarial_fullregex}). Contributions: (i) a one-line rule matching a 5-feature pipeline at 80.1\% (Haiku pooled-LOO) / 64.7\% (7-target cross-family) average; (ii) a decomposition of instruction-following vs.\ knowledge-conflict via three reusable controls (equalization, cross-family extraction, regex baselines), framed as what behavioral-deception work should report going forward; and (iii) negative scaling, adversarial, and autonomy results that together bound where the remaining signal lives. We frame the rule as a baseline against which more robust detectors should be measured, not as a deployment-ready detector. All behavioral detection here is English-only; refusal-marker patterns are not validated on non-English prompts or stylistic variants.
\end{abstract}
