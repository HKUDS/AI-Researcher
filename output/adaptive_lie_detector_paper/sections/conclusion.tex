\section{Conclusion}

We presented ADAGE, an adaptive interrogation framework for instructed lie detection in LLMs, and used it to characterize why black-box behavioral probing fails to reliably detect when a model is lying.

Our central finding is negative: when instruction asymmetry is removed (prompt-equalized control), classification accuracy drops from 93.9--100\% to 64--71\% across Llama~3.2~3B and Mistral~7B, with all five behavioral features collapsing and residual signal resting on a single fragile feature (defensiveness, $|d|\leq1.12$). The instructed-condition pipeline primarily detects how models comply with different instructions, not deceptive behavior per se.

Three additional findings sharpen this characterization. First, Claude Haiku's 97.8\% LOO accuracy is driven by 100\% refusal in lying trials---the classifier detects RLHF safety compliance. Second, cross-family extraction (Mistral scoring all 92 Claude Haiku transcripts, EXP-A) confirms same-family extractor inflation: defensiveness $d$ drops from 4.90 to 0.63 and LOO from 97.8\% to 82.6\%; the residual 82.6\% (above the equalized 64--71\% range) reflects RLHF refusal language visible to any extractor. Third, a simple hedging-word regex baseline achieves 86--94\% LOO, narrowing the gap to the full LLM-extracted pipeline to 4--14\,pp, showing most signal resides in surface lexical patterns. Held-out evaluation (50/50 splits, 100 seeds) and permutation tests ($p<0.001$, all models) confirm LOO results are genuine, not overfitting artifacts.

Behavioral mode classification via black-box interrogation is a tractable proxy for studying detection limits, and the multi-turn pipeline with LOO recalibration achieves strong instructed-condition accuracy (93.9--100\%). But the equalized controls establish that this reflects instruction-following detection, not deception detection. Future work should develop knowledge-probing approaches that do not rely on prompt asymmetries, use cross-family extractors by default, and evaluate under naturalistic rather than instructed deception scenarios.
