\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We demonstrate that behavioral deception detection in large language models primarily detects instruction-following artifacts rather than deception per se. Prompt equalization drops accuracy from 93.9--100\% (LOO-recalibrated) to 52--69\%, with instruction-following contributing $+$7.5--15\,pp on knowledge-controlled subsets (14B/70B).

The most actionable finding is a label-free \emph{baseline against which more robust detectors should be measured}: the rule refusal count $\geq 1$ achieves 80.1\% average accuracy (range: 64--88\%; 75--88\% on models $\geq$7B) across seven equalized models without calibration data. Simple regex baselines match or exceed the 5-feature LLM pipeline on 6 of 7 models, establishing that the residual knowledge-conflict signal is surface-level. The structured ADAGE pipeline adds little over these simple heuristics: $K=1$ suffices under instructed conditions ($+$1--3\,pp for multi-turn), and the refusal-count rule matches or exceeds the full 5-feature LLM pipeline under equalization. Multi-turn interrogation adds genuine value only under equalization at $\geq$14B scale ($+$19--21.5\,pp). The rule detects surface-lexical correction markers and inherits the fragility of RLHF-shaped behavior: it collapses on models where RLHF suppresses correction language (Qwen~32B: 50\%, at chance) and, as Section~\ref{sec:adversarial_paraphrase} shows, a one-shot adversarial paraphrase prompt drops rule accuracy from 64\% to 56\%~on Llama 3B ($-$8\,pp; mean refusal markers in deceptive trials fall from 1.74 to 0.24). A unified picture emerges: refusal-count signal weakens whenever the target is \emph{trained or instructed to be agreeable}---Qwen~32B's post-training smoothing, the adversarial paraphrase prompt, and the autonomous-persona and false-belief conditions (66\% and 60\% respectively) all instantiate the same failure mode. Deployment therefore requires domain-specific validation and monitoring; we present the rule as a baseline against which more robust detectors should be measured, not as a production-ready classifier.

Within-family scale analysis suggests family-specific, non-monotonic patterns: Qwen~2.5 exhibits a U-shape peaking at 14B with collapse at 32B (zero refusal markers due to RLHF agreeableness); Llama exhibits flat-then-jump (refusal-count LOO: 57\%$\to$57\%$\to$76\%; LLM pipeline: 61\%$\to$65\%$\to$84\%), with a single statistically significant increment (8B$\to$70B, $p=0.004$) under joint correction. The Qwen~32B collapse---where RLHF optimization entirely eliminates surface-level detection signals despite the model retaining factual knowledge---is a striking single-model observation that we report as a hypothesis-generator pending replication on additional RLHF-tuned models. Frontier-scale (100B+) behavior remains untested.

All refusal-marker patterns reported here are English-only and have not been validated on non-English prompts, code-switched inputs, or stylistic variants; any downstream use should be read with this scoping. Future work on behavioral detection should report results under all three controls (prompt equalization, cross-family extraction, regex baselines) to separate genuine signal from artifacts. Knowledge-probing approaches that build on the group-level gap in correction behavior may prove more fruitful than multi-turn behavioral interrogation. Specifically, questions designed to elicit correction behavior---presenting false alternatives and measuring whether models correct them unprompted---could yield features more robust to RLHF smoothing than the refusal-count markers that collapse at Qwen~32B.
