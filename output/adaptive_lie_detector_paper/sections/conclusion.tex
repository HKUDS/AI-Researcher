\section{Conclusion}

We used the ADAGE adaptive interrogation pipeline to systematically evaluate where and why black-box behavioral probing succeeds or fails at detecting instructed deception in LLMs. The primary contribution is three reusable methodological controls --- prompt equalization, cross-family extraction, and surface-level baselines --- and the empirical findings they enable.

Our central finding is that behavioral detection under controlled conditions is largely an instruction-following artifact, but with a meaningful scale-dependent residual. Removing instruction asymmetry (prompt-equalized control) drops classification accuracy from 93.9--100\% to 52--69\% under independent cross-family extraction, and 61--84\% under Claude extraction (which reflects both same-family bias for Claude targets and generally higher extractor capability). Under Claude extraction, equalized accuracy exhibits a two-tier structure: models $\leq$7B cluster at 61--74\% while models $\geq$14B cluster at 82.5--84\% (group difference $p < 0.0001$; no individual adjacent increment significant after Holm-Bonferroni correction); a within-family control (Qwen~7B: 74\% vs.\ Qwen~14B: 82.5\%) confirms this structure holds within a single model family. Cross-family extraction (Mistral Large) yields 52\% $\to$ 62\% $\to$ 69\% across 3B, 7B, and 70B. An instructed-matched control on five models (3B, 8B, 7B, 14B, 70B) decomposes the gap: instruction-following contributes $+$7.5--31\,pp, the dominant confound at all five scales, though on the three smaller models ($\leq$8B) this estimate conflates instruction-following with knowledge transfer (Section~5). Multi-turn interrogation is marginal under instructed conditions (+1--3\,pp) but essential under equalization: the $K=1$-to-adaptive gap grows from 0\,pp (3B) to +21.5\,pp (70B), indicating that multi-turn probing adds genuine value only when the instruction-following shortcut is removed. However, a hedging-word regex baseline achieves 87--91\% on models $\geq$14B equalized data, indicating that the detectable signal remains surface-level even at scale.

Three additional findings sharpen this characterization. First, Claude Haiku's 97.8\% LOO accuracy under instructed conditions is driven by 100\% refusal in lying trials---the classifier detects RLHF safety compliance. A full prompt-equalized evaluation ($n=99$) confirms: under neutral prompts, Haiku \emph{corrects} false claims rather than refusing, and LOO drops to 82.8\% (CI [74.7\%, 89.9\%], within the equalized range), confirming that the instructed result was primarily compliance detection. Second, cross-family extraction using two independent extractors (Mistral Large~3 and Qwen~2.5~14B) confirms the 3B$\to$70B scale trend under both, with accuracy 9--16\,pp lower than Claude extraction; however, Qwen breaks monotonicity at 7B, indicating extractor sensitivity. Third, a simple hedging-word regex baseline achieves 86--94\% LOO on instructed data, narrowing the gap to the full LLM-extracted pipeline to 4--14\,pp. Held-out evaluation (50/50 splits, 100 seeds) and permutation tests ($p<0.001$, all models) confirm LOO results are genuine, not overfitting artifacts.

The three controls we introduce --- prompt equalization, cross-family extraction, and hedging baselines --- are not novel in isolation (matched controls, cross-evaluator checks, and simple baselines are standard evaluation methodology), but their systematic application to behavioral deception detection exposes confounds of surprising magnitude: 30--41\,pp accuracy collapse under equalization, 7$\times$ defensiveness inflation under same-family extraction (EXP-A). No prior behavioral detection work controlled for these effects. The two-tier scale trend (52--69\% cross-family-extracted; 61--84\% Claude-extracted) identifies a genuine knowledge-conflict signal that is reliably stronger on models $\geq$14B than on models $\leq$7B ($p < 0.0001$ for the group comparison), though the precise scale at which the transition occurs cannot be localized from these data because the two bins differ in family composition as well as scale. Future work should develop knowledge-probing approaches that leverage this scale-dependent correction behavior, use cross-family extractors by default, exploit multi-turn probing under equalized conditions where it provides up to +21.5\,pp over single-question baselines, and evaluate under naturalistic rather than instructed deception scenarios.
